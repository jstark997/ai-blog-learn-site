## Who writes this

This site is written by Jeff Stark. Everything on it — the essays, the lessons
and the interactive demonstrations — is one person's work, published from a Git
repository with no editorial process behind it beyond that.

The paragraph that says who that is, and why anyone should read him on the
subject, is the author's to write.

## Why it exists

Writing about how a system works is the most reliable way to find out whether
you actually understand it. Most of what is published about artificial
intelligence sits at one of two extremes: commentary that never touches the
mechanics, or papers and documentation that assume you already know them.

This site tries to occupy the space between. The claim it makes is that the
interesting part of a machine learning system is usually its mechanism, and that
a mechanism can be explained honestly — with the mathematics that it needs, and
something you can move with your own hands.

## Who it is for

Readers who want to know how these systems work, and are willing to follow an
argument that includes a derivative.

That means some programming experience is assumed and some comfort with
mathematical notation helps, but nothing beyond first-year calculus and linear
algebra is required. Where a lesson needs more than that, it says so in its
prerequisites.

## Blog and Learn

The two halves of the site are written for different kinds of reading.

[The blog](/blog) is for ideas, observations and commentary: what a result
means, why an approach works, where the field seems to be going. Posts are
dated, stand on their own, and are read newest first.

[Learn](/learn) is for explanation. Lessons are grouped by topic and arranged in
reading order rather than by date, each one building on the last, with
interactive demonstrations you work through rather than only read about. A
lesson is revised when it is wrong or unclear — it is a reference, not a
dispatch.

If you are new here and want the mechanics, start with Learn. If you want the
argument, start with the blog.
